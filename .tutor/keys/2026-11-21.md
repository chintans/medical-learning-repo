## Warm-up
- **W1 B** Latin *os/ossis*, Greek *osteon* = bone. Tests the Latin–Greek pair item from the queue (S2). A cartilage = chondr-; C joint = arthr-; D muscle = my-.
- **W2 B** haem- (blood) + angi- (vessel) + -oma (mass) = a lump made of blood vessels. The word alone doesn't say benign or malignant. (In fact a liver haemangioma is the commonest benign liver tumour and usually needs no treatment, but the point here is that -oma alone doesn't tell you.) A is the "-oma = cancer" trap. ## Quiz
- **Q1 C** Thrombocytopenia (thrombocyte = platelet, -penia = too few) is a CBC result, so it's a test finding. Arthralgia, myalgia and "c/o fever" are what the patient reported (symptoms). ("Febrile" O/E would also be a finding, but it isn't an option.) Tests the 4-bucket sort (S3) and -penia (S1). The monsoon fever + low platelets pattern is why the doctor repeats the CBC (dengue among the causes; not needed for the answer).
- **Q2 A** Remove part of the colon = colectomy; bring the bowel out through the belly wall = colostomy (a stoma). B: -otomy = cut into, -scopy = look. C reverses the order. D: look, then cut into. Tests -ectomy/-ostomy (S1, S2 warm-up, S3).
- **Q3 A** k/c/o = known case of (current, established diagnosis); HTN = hypertension; s/p = status post (already had); chole- (bile) + cyst- (bladder) + -ectomy = gallbladder removal; OD = once daily. B: "used to have", -scopy, BD. C: "suspected", no surgery, SOS. D: hypo vs hyper, urinary bladder (cyst- alone), HS.
- **Q4 B** How drugs act and their adverse effects = pharmacology, a para-clinical subject (taught in MBBS Phase II). A physiology covers normal function, not drugs. C is clinical (treating real patients). D anatomy = structure. Tests the S1 layering.
- **Q5 B** hypo- (below) + glyc- (sugar) + -aemia (in the blood) = low blood sugar, measurable. arthr- + -algia = joint pain, patient-reported (symptom). An adverse-events table lists events that occurred in each arm; causation needs comparison with the control arm, and even then it is not proof for an individual event. A: the "table proves cause" trap. C: hyper vs hypo, and -algia vs -itis. D swaps them round. Tests -aemia, hypo-, -algia and the S2 "In the journal" point.

## Q6 model answer (grade for the idea)
"Read a medical word from the end: the suffix tells you what kind of thing it is, then the root tells you which body part. Splenomegaly = splen- (spleen) + -megaly (enlargement), so the spleen is enlarged. That's something the doctor found on examination or a scan, so it's a finding, not a diagnosis (it doesn't say why). Then sort each line of a note into symptom, finding, diagnosis or action. 'No dysuria' means the patient has no pain or difficulty passing urine (dys- = painful/difficult, -uria = urine). It's a finding (a negative one): the doctor checked for it and it wasn't there."
**Full credit:** the end-first rule + correct decode of splenomegaly + "no dysuria" = no painful urination AND placed as a finding (accept "negative finding"; also accept "symptom the patient denies" if he explains it was asked about and ruled out, since in strict clinical usage a denied symptom is recorded as a negative in the history — give full credit if the reasoning is clear).
**Half credit:** decodes both words correctly but no bucket logic, or calls splenomegaly a diagnosis.
**Zero:** decodes -megaly or dys-/-uria wrongly.
