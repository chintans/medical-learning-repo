## Quiz
- **Q1 A** Pre-clinical subjects cover normal structure and function. B is para-clinical, C is clinical (Phase III Part 2) and D is Phase III Part 1.
- **Q2 B** nephr- (kidney) + -ectomy (cut out) means the kidney was removed. A would be nephritis, C is nephrotomy or nephrolithotomy, and D is a scopy.
- **Q3 B** hepato- (liver) + -megaly (enlargement), plus hyper- (high) + bilirubin + -aemia (in blood). C confuses this with splenomegaly, A gets the "hyper" direction wrong, and D adds a diagnosis the report never stated.
- **Q4 A** s/p = status post (already done). Laparoscopic = keyhole, through small cuts in the abdominal wall. chole- (bile) + cyst (bladder) + -ectomy (removal) = gallbladder removal. tachy- (fast) + -cardia (heart) = fast heart rate. "Resolved" means it settled.
- **Q5 B** -ostomy = an opening made to the surface (a "stoma"). The colon is brought out through the belly wall and stool collects in a bag. "Temporary" means it is planned to be reversed later. A describes a colectomy alone, C is colonoscopy and D is a colotomy.

## Q6 model answer (grade for the idea, not the wording)
Pre-clinical teaching gives you the normal baseline: structure (anatomy), function (physiology) and chemistry (biochemistry). Para-clinical teaching explains what goes wrong and why (pathology, microbiology) and how drugs change the body (pharmacology). This only makes sense once you know what "normal" is. Clinical teaching applies both to real patients, linking symptoms, signs and tests to a cause and choosing treatment. Example: to understand why a diabetic patient gets thirsty, you need glucose handling by the kidney (physiology), then what high blood glucose does (pathology), then how metformin or insulin corrects it (pharmacology), and only then can you manage the patient (medicine).
**Full credit:** names what each layer contributes, shows the dependency, and gives a valid example of his own. **Half credit:** just lists the layers without the "why".
