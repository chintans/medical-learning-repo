## Part B — Recap card (5 min, nothing new)

### The big idea
Module 0.1 comes down to two skills: **decode a word** (read it from the end) and **sort a line** (symptom, finding, diagnosis or action). With those two you can read most of a case sheet or a discharge summary, and see what it leaves unexplained.

### The whole module on one card

```
DECODE A WORD: suffix first, then root, then prefix
   peri | card | itis      →  around · heart · inflammation
   hyper | bilirubin | aemia → above normal · bilirubin · in the blood

SORT A LINE into 4 buckets (plus "background" for k/c/o, s/p)
   SYMPTOM          FINDING                 DIAGNOSIS        ACTION
   c/o ..., -algia  O/E ..., test results,  Dx: ..., -itis   Rx, -ectomy,
   dys-             -megaly, "no X" lines   (a conclusion)   -scopy, advice
```

### The six traps worth remembering

| Trap | What's right |
|---|---|
| **-ectomy / -otomy / -ostomy** | remove / cut into / make an opening to the surface (a stoma) |
| **-oma** | a lump or collection. It never answers "is it cancer?" on its own (lipoma benign, carcinoma malignant, haematoma = trapped blood) |
| **-algia** | pain, a symptom. Arthr*algia* (joints hurt) is not arthr*itis* (joint inflamed) |
| **pneum-** | lung *and* air. Pneumothorax = air in the chest cavity, not a lung infection |
| **cyst-** alone | the urinary bladder. The gallbladder needs *chole-* in front |
| **"No X" lines** | they are findings: the doctor checked for something dangerous and it wasn't there |

### Terminology box
No new terms today except *angi-* (blood vessel), which is in the hint to W2 and has been added to `glossary.md`. Everything else is in the glossary from Sessions 1–3.

### In the doctor's office
Four questions that work on almost any report, whatever the specialty:
1. "Is that a **diagnosis**, or a **description** of what you're seeing?"
2. "Does the diagnosis explain **everything** in the report, or is anything separate?"
3. "Is this lump/finding **benign, malignant, or not known yet**? What would tell us?"
4. "What's the **plan**, and when should we come back?"

### In the journal
A paper's title, its inclusion/exclusion criteria and its adverse-events table are all written in this vocabulary. Decoding them tells you **what was studied, in whom, and what went wrong**. That is the first pass of reading any abstract, and it's where Module 1.S (biostatistics) will start.

## Part C — Quiz (6 min)

**Q1.** An OPD slip during the monsoon reads: *"c/o fever, arthralgia, myalgia × 4 days. O/E: febrile. CBC: thrombocytopenia. Advised repeat CBC tomorrow."* Which item is a **finding from a test**, rather than something the patient reported?
A. Arthralgia
B. Myalgia
C. Thrombocytopenia
D. "c/o fever"

**Q2.** A surgeon explains a plan for your friend's father: *"We'll remove the diseased part of the colon. If the join needs time to heal, we'll bring the bowel out through the belly wall for a few months."* Which pair of terms matches the two steps, in order?
A. Colectomy, then colostomy
B. Colotomy, then colonoscopy
C. Colostomy, then colectomy
D. Colonoscopy, then colotomy

**Q3.** Your father's file reads: *"k/c/o HTN. s/p cholecystectomy 2021. Tab. [BP medicine] OD."* The best plain-English reading is:
A. He has high blood pressure, had his gallbladder removed in 2021, and takes his BP tablet once a day
B. He used to have high blood pressure, had his gallbladder examined with a scope in 2021, and takes his tablet twice a day
C. He is suspected to have high blood pressure, had gallstones dissolved in 2021, and takes his tablet only when needed
D. He has low blood pressure, had his urinary bladder removed in 2021, and takes his tablet at bedtime

**Q4.** A second-year MBBS student is learning how a diabetes drug lowers blood sugar and why some patients get side effects from it. Which subject and layer is this?
A. Physiology, pre-clinical
B. Pharmacology, para-clinical
C. General Medicine, clinical
D. Anatomy, pre-clinical

**Q5.** The adverse-events table of a diabetes-drug trial lists "hypoglycaemia" and "arthralgia". Which reading is correct?
A. Both are diagnoses, and the table proves the drug caused them
B. Hypoglycaemia is low blood sugar (it can be measured); arthralgia is joint pain that patients reported. The table records what happened during the trial, not proof that the drug caused it
C. Hypoglycaemia is high blood sugar; arthralgia is inflamed joints seen on X-ray
D. Hypoglycaemia is a symptom patients describe; arthralgia is a blood-test result

**Q6 (short answer, 3–4 sentences).** Explain to a friend how to read medical language, as if they had never heard of it. Use the word **"splenomegaly"** (decode it; *splen-* = spleen) and the line **"No dysuria"** (say what it means and which bucket it goes in) as your two examples.
